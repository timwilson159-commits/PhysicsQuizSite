#1multiple-choicefrom Q21ainquiry 8.1
Which two properties of a star together determine its luminosity (total power output)?
  • ✓ Its size (radius or surface area) and its surface temperature
  • Its distance from Earth and its apparent brightness
  • Its rotational speed and its magnetic field strength
  • Its age and its chemical composition
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): $L \propto R^2 T^4$: a star is more luminous if it is larger or hotter.

In a DC motor, what is the function of the split-ring commutator (part X)?
  • ✓ It reverses the direction of the current through the rotating coil every half turn, so that the torque keeps turning the coil the same way
  • It steps the supply voltage up before it reaches the coil

#8multiple-choicefrom Q25binquiry 6.4
Why does the torque produced by a DC motor decrease as its rotational speed increases?
  • ✓ A faster-spinning coil generates a larger back emf, which reduces the net voltage across the coil and hence the current; torque is proportional to current
  • The magnetic field weakens as the coil spins faster
  • The commutator stops working at high speed
  • The coil's resistance increases with speed
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Back emf grows with rotational speed and opposes the supply voltage.

How are the composition and the surface temperature of a star each found from its spectrum?
  • ✓ Composition comes from the pattern of absorption (or emission) lines, since each element has its own characteristic set; temperature comes from the peak wavelength of the continuous spectrum (Wien's law)
  • Both come only from how bright the star appears

After the switch is closed, a second identical globe Y is added to the secondary circuit. Why does the current in the primary coil change?
  • ✓ The secondary now delivers more power (two globes instead of one), so by conservation of energy the primary must draw more current from the supply
  • The primary current falls, because the extra globe adds resistance to the primary circuit
  • The primary current is unchanged, because the transformer isolates the two circuits

#14multiple-choicefrom Q29inquiry 7.2
Light from an incandescent lamp is dimmed when passed through a single polarising filter. Why?
  • ✓ The lamp light is unpolarised (its electric field oscillates in all orientations); the filter transmits only the component aligned with its axis, which is on average half the intensity
  • The filter absorbs all wavelengths equally, halving the brightness of every colour
  • The filter reflects most of the light back towards the lamp
  • The filter slows the light down, reducing its energy
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Averaging $\cos^2\theta$ over all random orientations gives one half.
#15multiple-choicefrom Q30ainquiry 6.3
A complete copper ring is held near the end of an electromagnet. When the switch is turned on, the magnetic field through the ring rises from zero over about 0.05 s. What force acts on the ring during this time?
  • ✓ While the field is increasing, the induced current in the ring opposes the rising flux (Lenz's law), so the ring is pushed away from the coil; once the field becomes steady there is no induced current and no force
  • The ring is pulled towards the coil the whole time
  • There is no force at any time, because the ring is not connected to a battery
  • The ring is pushed away only after the field stops changing
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): A force appears only while the flux through the ring is changing.

  • ✓ An emf is still induced around the ring, but the gap breaks the circuit so no current can flow; with no current there is no force, and the ring stays still

  • The ring is pulled towards the coil

#17multiple-choicefrom Q31inquiry 6.3
A roller-coaster car brakes by passing magnets on the car either side of a fixed aluminium fin. The deceleration-time graph is recorded for entry speeds of 10 m s$^{-1}$ and 12 m s$^{-1}$. What do the two data sets have in common, and how do they differ?
  • ✓ Both show a deceleration that is largest at entry and then falls as the car slows (the eddy-current braking force depends on speed); the 12 m s$^{-1}$ run starts with a larger deceleration because the faster motion causes a faster change of flux
  • Both show a constant deceleration; the faster run just lasts longer
  • The slower run has the larger deceleration throughout
  • The two runs are identical, because magnetic braking does not depend on speed
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Eddy-current braking force grows with the relative speed, so it is strongest at entry and fades as the car slows.
