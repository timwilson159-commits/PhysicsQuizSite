  • Using a weaker permanent magnet

How did de Broglie's hypothesis address a limitation of the Bohr-Rutherford model of the atom?
  • ✓ Bohr simply assumed that only certain orbits were allowed; de Broglie explained this by treating the electron as a wave, so an allowed orbit is one whose circumference fits a whole number of electron wavelengths (a standing wave)

#11multiple-choicefrom Q26inquiry 7.4
Muons produced high in the atmosphere reach the ground even though, classically, they should decay first. How does relativity explain this from each frame of reference?
  • ✓ From Earth's frame, the fast muon's lifetime is dilated (stretched), so it lasts long enough to reach the ground; from the muon's frame, the distance to the ground is length-contracted (shortened), so it covers it in time
  • From both frames, the muons simply travel faster than light
  • The muons are replaced by new muons continuously, so some always reach the ground
  • Earth's magnetic field speeds the muons up as they fall
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Time dilation and length contraction are two views of the same result: the muon makes the trip within its lifetime.

#14multiple-choicefrom Q27cinquiry 7.4
A classical calculation gives each pion a speed greater than $3 \times 10^{8}$ m s$^{-1}$. What is the problem, and how is it resolved?
  • ✓ Nothing with mass can reach or exceed the speed of light; using relativistic mechanics, the released energy goes into relativistic momentum (relativistic mass), so the actual speed stays just below c
  • The calculation is fine; pions really do travel faster than light because they are so light
  • The problem is solved by ignoring the kinetic energy of the pions
  • The pions must actually be photons, which travel at exactly c
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): The classical formula $KE = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ fails near c; the relativistic version keeps $v < c$ no matter how much energy is added.

#18multiple-choicefrom Q30inquiry 5.2
An object rides on the floor of a rotating hollow cylinder, undergoing uniform circular motion. If the rotation period is halved, what happens to the forces on the object (ignore friction)?
  • ✓ The angular velocity doubles, so the centripetal acceleration ($\omega^2 r$) quadruples; the inward normal force from the wall, which supplies it, also quadruples, while the object's weight and the floor's support force are unchanged
  • All the forces on the object double
  • The centripetal force halves, because the period halved
  • None of the forces change, because the object's mass and radius are the same
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Centripetal acceleration is $\dfrac{4\pi^2 r}{T^2}$, so halving T multiplies it by 4.
#19multiple-choicefrom Q33inquiry 6.3
A magnet swings as a pendulum just above a freely rotating aluminium can. What happens to the can and the magnet?
  • ✓ The moving magnet induces eddy currents in the can; by Lenz's law these drag the can into rotation, following the magnet. The magnet loses mechanical energy (transformed into electrical energy and then heat in the can), so its swing is damped
  • The can is unaffected because aluminium is not magnetic, and the magnet swings forever
  • The can spins up while the magnet's swing grows larger and larger
  • The magnet is repelled straight upward and never swings back
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Eddy-current coupling transfers energy from the magnet's motion to the can and then to heat, so total energy is conserved while the magnet slows.
