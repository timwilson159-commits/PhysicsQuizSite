#1numeric-entryfrom Q21ainquiry 5.1
An arrow is fired and stays airborne for 4.3 s, landing at the same height it was launched, with a horizontal range of 205 m. Calculate the magnitude of the arrow's initial velocity. Use $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.
52.2m s^{-1}
accepted within ±3% (relative)
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Horizontal: $u_x = \dfrac{205}{4.3}$. Vertical: the arrow takes 2.15 s to reach the top, so $u_y = g \times 2.15$. Then $u = \sqrt{u_x^2 + u_y^2}$.

#3multiple-choicefrom Q22ainquiry 6.4
A hand-cranked device has a coil that spins between the poles of a magnet, with its ends connected to two slip rings and then to an external circuit. What is this device?
  • ✓ An AC generator: turning the coil in the field induces an alternating emf, and the slip rings deliver AC to the circuit
  • A DC motor: the slip rings act as a commutator to keep the coil turning one way
  • A transformer: the spinning coil steps the voltage up
  • An electromagnet: the current in the coil creates a steady magnetic field
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Slip rings (rather than a split-ring commutator) and a mechanical input are the signature of an AC generator.
#4numeric-entryfrom Q22binquiry 6.4
A generator coil of 200 turns, each of area $0.10 \times 0.05$ m$^2$, sits in a 0.8 T field. Calculate the average emf induced when the coil is turned through a quarter of a revolution (from face-on to edge-on) in 18 ms.
44.4V
accepted within ±4% (relative)
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Flux per turn goes from $\Phi = BA$ to 0, so $\varepsilon = N\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t} = 200 \times \dfrac{0.8 \times (0.10 \times 0.05)}{0.018}$.
#5true-falsefrom Q22cinquiry 6.4
In a generator, the back emf produced by the spinning coil opposes the rotation, so more mechanical work must be put in than electrical energy is taken out. This is how the generator obeys conservation of energy.
  • ✓ True
  • False
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): If drawing more current makes the generator harder to turn, the extra electrical energy is being paid for by extra mechanical work.
#6multiple-choicefrom Q24binquiry 5.2
A farmer wants to maximise electricity generation from a waterwheel. There are two designs (a flat-paddle wheel and a bucket wheel) available in 2 m, 3 m and 4 m diameters. Which choice gives the greatest torque, and therefore the most power?
  • ✓ The bucket wheel at 4 m diameter: the water's weight in the buckets adds force, and the larger radius increases the torque arm
  • The flat-paddle wheel at 2 m diameter: a smaller wheel spins faster
  • The flat-paddle wheel at 4 m diameter: paddles catch more water than buckets
  • The bucket wheel at 2 m diameter: a compact wheel is more efficient
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Torque is $\tau = rF\sin\theta$; both a larger force and a larger radius increase it.

#8multiple-choicefrom Q27ainquiry 6.3
A transformer used in a power-transmission grid has many more turns on its secondary coil than its primary coil. What type of transformer is it, and where is it used?
  • ✓ A step-up transformer, used at the power station to raise the voltage for long-distance transmission
  • A step-down transformer, used at the power station
  • A step-up transformer, used in each home

#9multiple-choicefrom Q28ainquiry 8.4
Which type(s) of radioactive decay are transmutation reactions (a change from one element into another)?
  • ✓ Alpha and beta decay, but not gamma decay
  • Gamma decay only
  • All three: alpha, beta and gamma decay

#14numeric-entryfrom Q31binquiry 6.1
A beta particle (an electron) enters a magnetic field of strength $6.7 \times 10^{-4}$ T at right angles, travelling at 0.4c. Calculate the radius of its circular path. Use electron mass $9.1 \times 10^{-31}$ kg, charge $1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C and $c = 3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m s$^{-1}$.
1.02m
accepted within ±5% (relative)
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): The magnetic force provides the centripetal force: $qvB = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$, so $r = \dfrac{mv}{qB}$ with $v = 0.4c$.

In the Geiger-Marsden experiment, most alpha particles passed straight through the gold foil but a very small number were deflected through large angles. What did Rutherford conclude?
  • ✓ The atom is mostly empty space, with its positive charge and almost all its mass concentrated in a tiny central nucleus
