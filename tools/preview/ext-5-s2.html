#4multiple-choicefrom Q23inquiry 6.2
A vertical wire carrying a current is placed just in front of the middle of a permanent bar magnet. When the current is switched on, why does the wire move?
  • ✓ The current-carrying wire is in the magnet's field, so it experiences a motor-effect force ($F = BIl$) directed perpendicular to both the current and the field
  • The wire becomes magnetised and is attracted to the nearest pole
  • The changing current induces an emf in the magnet, which repels the wire
  • The wire heats up and expands towards the magnet
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): This is the motor effect: a current in an external magnetic field feels a sideways force.

The electrician proposes using an 11 000 V supply with a step-down transformer at the house instead of the 240 V supply, to deliver the same power with far less line loss. Why does the higher supply voltage reduce the power lost in the line?
  • ✓ For the same power, a higher voltage means a proportionally smaller current, and line loss depends on the square of the current
  • A higher voltage pushes the electricity through the wires faster, so less is lost
  • The transformer removes the resistance of the transmission line
  • Higher voltage lines are made of a more conductive metal
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): $P = VI$ fixes the current for a given power; line loss is $I^2 R$, so cutting the current by 46 times cuts the loss by about 2000 times.

Which observation from the photoelectric effect could NOT be explained by the wave model of light?
  • ✓ No photoelectrons are emitted below a threshold frequency, no matter how intense or how long the light is applied
  • Brighter light produces a larger photoelectric current
  • Photoelectrons travel through the circuit to the collecting plate

A 100 kg satellite is in a near-circular orbit of radius $6.671 \times 10^{6}$ m around Earth. Calculate its orbital period. Use $G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}$ N m$^2$ kg$^{-2}$ and Earth's mass $6.0 \times 10^{24}$ kg.
