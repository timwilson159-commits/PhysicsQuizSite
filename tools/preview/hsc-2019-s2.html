#1multiple-choicefrom Q21inquiry 8.3
Which statement best outlines de Broglie's contribution to quantum mechanics?
  • ✓ He proposed that any particle of momentum $p$ has an associated wavelength $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p}$, giving matter a wave nature
  • He showed that light is emitted in discrete quanta of energy $E = hf$
  • He derived an equation for the probability of finding an electron in a region of space

💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): His idea explained why electrons in a Bohr atom form standing waves, and it was confirmed by electron diffraction.
#2multiple-choicefrom Q22inquiry 8.1
Besides chemical composition and surface temperature, which additional feature of a star can be found from its spectrum?
  • ✓ Its rotational speed, from the broadening of its spectral lines caused by one edge approaching and the other receding
  • Its exact age in years, read directly off the spectrum
  • Its distance from Earth, from the total brightness of the spectrum
  • The number of planets orbiting it, from the number of dark lines
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): A rotating star has one limb blueshifted and the other redshifted, which smears each line out; an overall shift of all lines instead gives the star's radial velocity.

4.0eV

#4numeric-entryfrom Q24ainquiry 6.3
A transformer is supplied with 500 J s$^{-1}$ of electrical energy. Its secondary delivers 50 V at 9 A. Calculate the efficiency of the transformer as a percentage.
90%
accepted within ±3% (relative)
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Output power = $V_s I_s = 50 \times 9$; efficiency = $\dfrac{\text{output power}}{\text{input power}} \times 100$.

#7multiple-choicefrom Q26inquiry 5.2
A student applies a 400 N force to a spanner at 25 cm from the bolt, at various angles, and finds the torque is closely proportional to the angle ($\tau = k\theta$) up to about 25 degrees, then the model becomes less accurate. Why?
  • ✓ The true torque is $\tau = rF\sin\theta$; for small angles $\sin\theta \approx \theta$ (in radians), but as the angle grows the sine curve flattens and rises more slowly than a straight line
  • The force gets weaker as the angle increases
  • The spanner bends at larger angles, shortening the lever arm
  • Friction at the bolt increases with angle
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Compare a straight line through the origin with the graph of $\sin\theta$: they agree near zero and diverge as $\theta$ approaches 90 degrees.

  • Light from distant galaxies is redshifted in proportion to distance

#9multiple-choicefrom Q29inquiry 6.1
A particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ is accelerated from rest through a potential difference $V$. The result $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2qV}{m}}$ is obtained by:
  • ✓ Setting the electrical work done on the particle, $qV$, equal to its gain in kinetic energy, $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$
  • Setting the electric force $qE$ equal to the magnetic force $qvB$
  • Equating the particle's momentum before and after acceleration
  • Using $v = u + at$ with $u = 0$ and $a = g$
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Work-energy theorem: all the work done by the field becomes kinetic energy.
#10numeric-entryfrom Q30ainquiry 5.1
A ball starts from rest at P and slides down a frictionless track, leaving the track at Q with a speed of 1.5 m s$^{-1}$. Calculate the height of P above Q. Use $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.
0.11m
accepted within ±6% (relative)
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Energy conservation on the frictionless track: $mg\Delta h = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$, so $\Delta h = \dfrac{v^2}{2g}$.
#11numeric-entryfrom Q30binquiry 5.1
The ball leaves the track at Q with a velocity of 1.5 m s$^{-1}$ directed 50 degrees above the horizontal, and reaches the floor 0.5 s later. Calculate the height of Q above the floor. Use $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.
0.65m
accepted within ±6% (relative)
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Take up as positive: $u_y = 1.5\sin50^\circ$. The vertical displacement is $u_y t - \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2$ (a negative number); the height of Q is its magnitude.
#12true-falsefrom Q31inquiry 6.4
An electric ceiling fan hangs from a spring balance. When the fan is switched on and speeds up, the reading on the spring balance decreases, because the fan pushes air downward and, by Newton's third law, the air pushes upward on the fan.
  • ✓ True
  • False

#13multiple-choicefrom Q33inquiry 6.1
A proton and an alpha particle are fired into a uniform magnetic field with the same speed, perpendicular to the field. Which statement correctly compares their paths?
  • ✓ Both follow circular arcs, but the alpha particle's radius is larger because its mass-to-charge ratio is about twice the proton's
  • Both follow circular arcs of exactly the same radius
  • The proton travels in a straight line while the alpha particle curves
  • The alpha particle's radius is smaller because it is more massive
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): $r = \dfrac{mv}{qB}$; for the alpha particle $m$ is about 4 times and $q$ is 2 times the proton's, so $\dfrac{m}{q}$ doubles.

#16numeric-entryfrom Q36inquiry 8.4
A stationary radon-198 atom (197.999 u) undergoes alpha decay to polonium-194 (193.988 u) plus a helium-4 nucleus (4.00260 u). The polonium atom is produced with 2.55 x 10^-14 J of kinetic energy. Using the mass defect, calculate the kinetic energy carried away by the alpha particle. Use 1 u = 1.661 x 10^-27 kg and c = 3.0 x 10^8 m s^-1.
1.23e-12J
accepted within ±5% (relative)
💡 Tip (shown only if the student answers incorrectly): Mass defect = 197.999 - 193.988 - 4.00260 u; energy released = (mass defect) x c^2. The alpha particle gets what is left after the polonium's kinetic energy.
